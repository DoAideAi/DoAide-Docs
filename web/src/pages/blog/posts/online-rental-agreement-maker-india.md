## What a rental agreement must cover

A usable agreement answers every question that could later become an argument:

1. **Parties and property** — full names of landlord and tenant(s), and the complete address of the premises.
2. **Term** — start date and duration (typically 11 months), with the renewal understanding.
3. **Rent** — amount, due date, payment mode, and the annual escalation (5–10% is customary in Indian cities).
4. **Security deposit** — amount, that it is refundable, permitted deductions, and the refund timeline after vacating (specify a number of days; "soon" is not a clause).
5. **Lock-in period** — the months during which neither side can exit without consequence, if any.
6. **Notice period** — usually one to two months, for both sides.
7. **Maintenance and repairs** — who pays society maintenance; minor repairs (tenant) vs structural repairs (landlord).
8. **Utilities** — electricity, water, gas, internet: in whose name and who pays.
9. **Use restrictions** — residential use only, subletting prohibited without consent, any rules on pets or alterations.
10. **Fittings list** — an annexure listing fans, geysers, furniture with condition noted, signed by both sides; this single page prevents most deposit disputes.

## Why are rent agreements 11 months long?

The Registration Act, 1908 requires **compulsory registration of leases of 12 months or longer**. An 11-month agreement stays below that line, avoiding the registration fee and office visit, which is why the 11-month term became the Indian default. It is renewed (often with the customary rent escalation) when the term ends.

Is 11 months always right? For most urban tenancies, yes — it is cheap, fast and legally sound. For long commitments (a company lease, a 3-year term with heavy fit-out costs), a **registered long-term lease** gives both sides stronger, court-ready protection and is worth the fee.

## Stamp duty, notarisation and registration — untangled

These three get mixed up constantly:

- **Stamp duty** is a state tax on the agreement itself. Every rental agreement should be executed on stamp paper (or franked/e-stamped) of the value set by your state — commonly ₹100–₹500 for 11-month agreements, with some states charging a percentage of annual rent. E-stamping portals (such as SHCIL) or stamp vendors supply it.
- **Notarisation** is a notary attesting the signatures. It is **not legally required** for an 11-month agreement and does not substitute for registration; many landlords still prefer it as signature evidence. It costs a nominal amount.
- **Registration** at the sub-registrar's office is **mandatory only for terms of 12 months or more** (and is standard practice regardless of term in some states' rental portals — for example, Maharashtra's online registration for leave-and-licence agreements). Registration involves a fee plus both parties' (or their representatives') biometrics/signatures at the office or via the state's online process.

The practical default for an 11-month tenancy: **print on the correct stamp paper, both parties sign with two witnesses, done.** Notarise if either side wants the extra comfort; check your state's rules — stamp values and registration practice vary by state.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an online rental agreement legally valid in India?

Yes. An agreement drafted online, printed on the correct stamp paper and signed by both parties with witnesses is as valid as one drafted by a lawyer. The drafting tool only changes who typed it; execution on stamp paper is what matters.

### Can I make a rent agreement without a lawyer?

For a standard residential tenancy, yes — a complete template covering rent, deposit, notice and maintenance handles the common cases. Involve a lawyer for unusual situations: commercial premises, very large deposits, long registered leases or ongoing disputes.

### What is the stamp duty on a rent agreement?

It varies by state — commonly a flat ₹100–₹500 for 11-month residential agreements, while some states calculate a percentage of the annual rent. Check your state's e-stamping portal for the exact value before printing.

### Is notarisation of a rent agreement mandatory?

No. Notarisation is optional attestation of signatures and is not a legal requirement for an 11-month agreement, nor a substitute for registration where registration is required. Many parties still notarise for comfort.

### Does an 11-month rent agreement need registration?

No — registration is compulsory only for leases of 12 months or longer under the Registration Act. Note that some states (like Maharashtra for leave-and-licence agreements) have their own registration practice even for shorter terms, so check local rules.

### Can the agreement be signed digitally?

Aadhaar-based e-sign and digital execution are increasingly accepted, and some state portals complete the whole process online. The traditional print-stamp-sign route remains the most universally accepted, especially where documents may be shown to societies, police verification or banks.
