- **Legal disputes**: An experience letter missing the designation or tenure period can lead to disputes during background verification.

## The 10 essential HR documents

### 1. Offer letter

The first formal document a new hire receives. It confirms the role, compensation, joining date, and conditions of employment. Indian offer letters should mention:

- Designation and department
- CTC breakdown (basic, HRA, special allowance, PF, gratuity)
- Joining date and probation period
- Reporting manager
- Conditions (background check, medical fitness)

### 2. Appointment letter

Issued on or before the joining date, the appointment letter is the legally binding employment contract. Unlike the offer letter, it includes detailed terms — notice period, leave policy, confidentiality clauses, and termination conditions.

Key elements under the Shops and Establishments Act and state-specific labour laws:

- Employee and employer details
- Terms of employment (probation, confirmation criteria)
- Compensation structure
- Leave entitlements
- Notice period and termination clauses

### 3. Salary slip

Mandatory under the Payment of Wages Act, 1936. Every employer must issue a monthly salary slip showing earnings and deductions. A compliant Indian salary slip includes:

- **Earnings**: Basic salary, HRA, conveyance, special allowance, medical allowance
- **Deductions**: PF (employee + employer), ESI, professional tax, TDS
- **Net pay** and payment mode

### 4. Experience letter

Issued when an employee leaves, confirming their tenure, designation, and (optionally) performance. Banks, visa offices, and future employers routinely ask for this.

Must include:

- Full name and employee ID
- Designation held and department
- Date of joining and last working day
- Brief mention of conduct and performance (optional but standard)
- Company letterhead and authorised signatory

## Best practices for HR document management

1. **Use consistent formatting**: Same font, logo placement, and layout across all documents. This builds trust and looks professional.
2. **Include company letterhead**: Every document should carry the company name, logo, registered address, and CIN/GSTIN where applicable.
3. **Get authorised signatures**: Ensure documents are signed by the appropriate authority — HR head, department manager, or director.
4. **Maintain a register**: Keep a log of all documents issued — employee name, document type, date, and reference number.
5. **Issue on time**: Experience letters and relieving letters should be issued on the last working day. Salary slips should go out with each pay cycle.
6. **Store digitally**: Keep PDF copies of all issued documents for at least 8 years (as recommended by the Shops and Establishments Act retention periods).

## Legal requirements to keep in mind

- **Payment of Wages Act, 1936**: Mandates salary slips for all employees earning below ₹24,000/month (many companies extend this to all employees).
- **Industrial Disputes Act, 1947**: Requires appointment letters for workmen and documented warnings before termination.
- **Shops and Establishments Act** (state-specific): Most states require employers to issue appointment letters within 30 days of joining.
- **Labour Codes 2020** (once fully notified): The Code on Wages will mandate salary slips for all employees; the Industrial Relations Code will require appointment letters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it mandatory to give an appointment letter in India?

Yes, under most state Shops and Establishments Acts and the upcoming Industrial Relations Code, employers must issue an appointment letter. Even where not strictly mandated, it is considered a best practice and protects both parties.

### How long should a company retain HR documents?

Most states recommend retaining employee records for 5–8 years after the employee's exit. Salary records, PF records, and tax documents should be kept for at least 8 years.

### What is the difference between an offer letter and an appointment letter?

An offer letter is a preliminary document expressing intent to hire, often conditional on background checks and medical fitness. An appointment letter is the formal, binding employment contract issued on or before the joining date with detailed terms and conditions.
